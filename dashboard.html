Fort Myers Beach Hurricane Ian Recovery Dashboard

The Bottom Line

Three and a half years after Hurricane Ian, Fort Myers Beach's recovery is real but uneven. Lodging capacity has rebuilt to 66% of pre-Ian inventory (Chamber, March 2026), while bed-tax revenue has reached 48% (FY25 YTD through Aug 2025) — the gap, currently 18 points with FY26 data pending, says rooms are coming back faster than per-room revenue. The barrier-island story remains distinct from the mainland: FMB at 48% bed-tax recovery is meaningfully ahead of Sanibel at 31%, while inland Lee County (Fort Myers mainland 143%, Estero 102%) has already moved past its pre-Ian baseline.

Capacity vs Revenue

Two strong indicators tell different parts of the same story. Lodging capacity is rebuilding faster than per-room revenue — the durable directional finding. As of the most recent shared data points, capacity (Chamber, March 2026) sits 18 points ahead of bed-tax revenue (FY25 YTD through August 2025). The specific gap will move as FY26 Q1 bed tax arrives from Lee County and as the tourist season completes; the direction is the recovery story behind the recovery story.

Scale context for the FMB–Sanibel comparison: FMB's pre-Ian lodging inventory (6,263 rooms) was nearly 2× Sanibel's (3,223). The hotel segments are comparable in size — both cities had roughly 1,800 hotel rooms pre-Ian and roughly 1,300 operating today. FMB's vacation-rental market is structurally larger: Sanibel's ordinance requires a 28-day minimum stay for single-family homes (7 days for most condos), while FMB's ordinance requires a 7-day minimum across the board — a regulatory difference that predates Ian and constrains the home-rental segment of Sanibel's STR market. The two cities are peer markets at the hotel level; FMB is a larger STR market overall.

Rebuilding Activity & Fiscal Context

Two related context signals shown here as the post-Ian wind-down picture. Permit and code-enforcement activity are declining — the expected pattern as the rebuilding surge winds down. Sales tax and disaster reimbursements are also declining — the parallel fiscal-pressure side: the recovery funding that supported the rebuild is tapering. Neither set is included in any recovery percentage on this dashboard — rebuilding activity is not the same as return to normalcy.

Where Things Stand — May 2026

Update Insight

Three and a half years after Hurricane Ian, Fort Myers Beach's recovery is real but uneven. Lodging capacity has rebuilt to 67% of pre-Ian inventory, while bed-tax revenue has reached 48% (FY25 YTD through Aug 2025) — the gap (currently 18 points, with FY26 Q1 bed tax pending) says rooms are coming back faster than per-room revenue. The barrier-island story remains distinct from the mainland: FMB at 48% bed-tax recovery is meaningfully ahead of Sanibel at 31%, while inland Lee County (Fort Myers mainland 143%, Estero 102%) has already moved past its pre-Ian baseline.

Recovery has been vacation-rental led, not hotel-led. VR rooms make up 68% of FMB's post-Ian lodging mix — a structural shift, not a temporary lag. The pipeline tells the next chapter: 1,175 new lodging units across 14 projects are in development, with one project's residents' lawsuit dismissed in May 2026 and another's LPA recommendation due before Council in June.

The hard limit on recovery is fixed: 816 rooms are permanently gone across 19 destroyed hotel properties and 6 destroyed VR complexes. No amount of rebuilding restores them — recovery above ~87% of pre-Ian capacity requires entirely new construction. The watch signals to follow over the next four quarters: the 160 delinquent VR licenses (does the count fall as owners return), the capacity-vs-revenue gap (does the 19-point spread close), and FY26 Q1 bed-tax data when Lee County releases it.

Where Things Stand — May 2026

Update Insight

Three and a half years after Hurricane Ian, Fort Myers Beach's recovery is real but uneven. Lodging capacity has rebuilt to 67% of pre-Ian inventory, while bed-tax revenue has reached 48% — the 19-point gap says rooms are coming back faster than per-room revenue. The barrier-island story remains distinct from the mainland: FMB at 48% bed-tax recovery is meaningfully ahead of Sanibel at 31%, while inland Lee County (Fort Myers mainland 143%, Estero 102%) has already moved past its pre-Ian baseline.

Recovery has been vacation-rental led, not hotel-led. VR rooms make up 68% of FMB's post-Ian lodging mix — a structural shift, not a temporary lag. The pipeline tells the next chapter: 1,175 new lodging units across 14 projects are in development, with one project's residents' lawsuit dismissed in May 2026 and another's LPA recommendation due before Council in June.

The hard limit on recovery is fixed: 753 rooms are permanently gone across 18 destroyed hotel properties and 6 destroyed VR complexes. No amount of rebuilding restores them — recovery above ~88% of pre-Ian capacity requires entirely new construction. The watch signals to follow over the next four quarters: the 160 delinquent VR licenses (does the count fall as owners return), the capacity-vs-revenue gap (does the 19-point spread close), and FY26 Q1 bed-tax data when Lee County releases it.

About the Data

Methodology, included metrics, and deliberate exclusions.

Methodology Rules

  1. Compare each metric to its pre-Ian baseline where available.
  2. Cap recovery percentage at 100% (exceeding baseline counts as fully recovered).
  3. When a baseline is not yet verified, mark "baseline pending" rather than fabricate a number.
  4. Exclude inverse indicators (e.g., real estate listing volume — high inventory signals distress, not recovery).
  5. Surface leading indicators (delinquent VR licenses, café closure concentration) as Watchlist, not in the headline.

What This May 2026 Update Includes

  • Bed tax recovery (48%): the revenue indicator — full 4-year YTD series (FY22–FY25 through August) from Lee County Clerk of Court.
  • Lodging capacity recovery (66%): hotel rooms (76%) and vacation rental rooms (63%) — direct from FMB Chamber of Commerce, March 2026.
  • Capacity vs Revenue analysis: the 19-point gap between capacity and revenue is featured as its own analytical section.
  • Sister-city comparison: bed-tax recovery side-by-side for FMB / Sanibel / Fort Myers / Estero.
  • Pipeline: 1,175 lodging units in development across 14 named projects (909 hotel + 266 VR). Chamber March 2026 baseline; sourced overrides per project; per-override audit trail in recovery-summary.md.
  • Permanent losses: 753 rooms lost forever (527 hotel + 226 VR) — sets the ceiling on recovery. Chamber's published count was 19 hotels / 590 rooms; sourced override removes Sandpiper Gulf Resort (reopened 2025) → 18 hotels / 527 rooms.
  • Economic activity: restaurants, retail, marinas, total businesses.
  • Watchlist signals: delinquent VR licenses, café closures, currently-closed businesses, population context.

Why Bed Tax is the Lead Metric

Bed tax holds the lead position on this dashboard because of four specific properties of the data:

  1. Independent audit chain. Lee County Clerk of Court collects and audits the Tourist Development Tax monthly. Neither this dashboard, the Chamber, nor the properties being tracked have access to alter the numbers. Every other metric here relies on Chamber surveys, a business-database scrape, or third-party reporting.
  2. Revenue, not just capacity. Hotel-room counts and VR licenses measure rooms that exist. Bed tax measures rooms that are operating × occupied × paid. A rebuilt-but-empty hotel doesn't move it. The bed-tax dollar is a composite of inventory, occupancy, rates, and season length, integrated into a single audited number.
  3. Cross-municipality comparable. The same TDT applies to every Lee County municipality on the same basis. The 48% / 31% / 143% / 102% comparison across FMB, Sanibel, mainland Fort Myers, and Estero is directly comparable — no other metric on this dashboard allows that.
  4. Continuous pre-Ian history. Monthly collections back through FY22 give a clean baseline. Most other FMB-specific pre-Ian baselines are still pending (housing, restaurants, retail).

Bed tax doesn't capture residential housing activity, day-tripper spending, or non-lodging retail — those are tracked in their own sections.

What's Excluded (and Why)

  • Real estate listings volume alone: high inventory can indicate distress as easily as health, so listing volume isn't used as a recovery indicator. Pre-Ian-vs-current listings + sales (split by property type) is the correct frame — see the Housing Market section.
  • Permits issued / closed: measures reconstruction activity, not return to normalcy.
  • Population without context: flat year-over-year ACS numbers can hide significant displacement and re-arrival; shown as context not as a recovery metric.

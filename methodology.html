Fort Myers Beach Recovery: May 2026 Update

How we track Hurricane Ian recovery on Fort Myers Beach.

The Bottom Line

Lodging capacity: 66% recovered (4,159 of 6,263 pre-Ian rooms — FMB Chamber, March 2026).

Bed tax revenue: 48% recovered (FY25 YTD through August vs FY22 YTD — Lee County Clerk).

The gap between capacity (Chamber, March 2026) and revenue (FY25 YTD through Aug 2025) — currently 18 points — means each rebuilt room is generating less revenue than its pre-Ian counterpart. The specific point-gap will be revised as FY26 Q1 bed tax data arrives from Lee County and as the tourist season completes.

FMB's distinctive recovery pattern: vacation rentals (2,853 rooms) make up the majority of rebuilt capacity. The post-Ian lodging mix is more VR-tilted than the pre-Ian mix — a structural shift, not a temporary lag. This is why FMB is outpacing Sanibel on bed-tax recovery despite Sanibel having a stronger overall economic-recovery profile.

Our Methodology

The rules used on this dashboard:

Five Rules

  1. Compare each metric to its pre-Ian baseline where the baseline has a verifiable source.
  2. Cap recovery at 100% — a metric that exceeds its baseline is counted as fully recovered, not "120% recovered."
  3. Mark "baseline pending" when data is unverified. Don't fabricate a number to fill a card.
  4. Exclude inverse indicators — high real-estate listing volume signals distress, not recovery.
  5. Surface leading indicators as Watchlist, not headline — the 5 currently-closed businesses are a small denominator; the 160 delinquent VR licenses are the bigger story.

Why Bed Tax Is the Lead Revenue Metric

Bed tax holds the lead position on this dashboard because of four specific properties of the data:

  1. Independent audit chain. Lee County Clerk of Court collects and audits the Tourist Development Tax monthly. Neither this dashboard, the Chamber, nor the properties being tracked have access to alter the numbers. Every other metric here relies on Chamber surveys, a business-database scrape, or third-party reporting.
  2. Revenue, not just capacity. Hotel-room counts and VR licenses measure rooms that exist. Bed tax measures rooms that are operating × occupied × paid. A rebuilt-but-empty hotel doesn't move it. The bed-tax dollar is a composite of inventory, occupancy, rates, and season length, integrated into a single audited number.
  3. Cross-municipality comparable. The same TDT applies to every Lee County municipality on the same basis. The 48% / 31% / 143% / 102% comparison across FMB, Sanibel, mainland Fort Myers, and Estero is directly comparable — no other metric on this dashboard allows that.
  4. Continuous pre-Ian history. Monthly collections back through FY22 give a clean baseline. Most other FMB-specific pre-Ian baselines are still pending (housing, restaurants, retail).

Bed tax doesn't capture residential housing activity, day-tripper spending, or non-lodging retail — those are tracked in their own sections.

Notes on the Numbers

Hotel room baseline

The Chamber's pre-Ian baseline of 1,724 rooms includes all properties operating immediately pre-Ian, plus the room counts at the 19 properties permanently destroyed. The 1,306 current rooms includes the new 254-room Margaritaville Beach Resort (opened post-Ian). At surviving pre-Ian properties, ~93% of room capacity is back online (1,052 of 1,134 surviving rooms); the remaining gap is the 590 rooms at the 19 destroyed properties.

Hotel destruction

19 hotel properties were permanently destroyed by Hurricane Ian, totaling 590 rooms. Notable losses include Outrigger Beach Resort (144 rooms), Wyndham Garden (103), Sandpiper Gulf Resort (63), Sandcastle Beach (53, Chamber-side uncertainty on room count), and Hideaway Village (23).

VR license interpretation

The 1,982 active vacation rental licenses (current) likely exceed the pre-Ian count — FMB's vacation rental market accelerated post-Ian as destroyed hotel rooms were replaced by short-term rentals. The 160 delinquent licenses are the more meaningful signal — properties that hold a license but cannot currently operate, typically because of ongoing damage or rebuild status.

Population

The 2024 ACS figure of 5,513 is close to the 2020 Census of 5,582, but the ACS also reports a 21.8% decline 2019–2024. This volatility reflects displacement and partial return, not stability. Population is shown as context, not as a recovery metric in the headline.

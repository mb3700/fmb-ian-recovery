Fort Myers Beach Recovery: Q1 2026 Baseline

How we track Hurricane Ian recovery on Fort Myers Beach.

The Bottom Line

Lodging capacity: 66% recovered (4,159 of 6,263 pre-Ian rooms — FMB Chamber, March 2026).

Bed tax revenue: 48% recovered (FY25 YTD through August vs FY22 YTD — Lee County Clerk).

The 18-point gap between capacity and revenue means each rebuilt room is generating less revenue than its pre-Ian counterpart.

Three-year bed-tax trajectory (YTD through August): 15% (FY23) → 37% (FY24) → 48% (FY25). Rising steadily.

Pipeline: 1,303 lodging rooms in development across 14 named projects (942 hotel + 361 VR).

FMB's distinctive recovery pattern: vacation rentals (2,853 rooms) make up the majority of rebuilt capacity. The post-Ian lodging mix is more VR-tilted than the pre-Ian mix — a structural shift, not a temporary lag. This is why FMB is outpacing Sanibel on bed-tax recovery despite Sanibel having a stronger overall economic-recovery profile.

Our Methodology

Adapted from the Sanibel Solutions recovery framework. The rules are deliberately strict to keep the dashboard credible.

Five Rules

  1. Compare each metric to its pre-Ian baseline where the baseline has a verifiable source.
  2. Cap recovery at 100% — a metric that exceeds its baseline is counted as fully recovered, not "120% recovered."
  3. Mark "baseline pending" when data is unverified. Don't fabricate a number to fill a card.
  4. Exclude inverse indicators — high real-estate listing volume signals distress, not recovery.
  5. Surface leading indicators as Watchlist, not headline — the 5 currently-closed businesses are a small denominator; the 160 delinquent VR licenses are the bigger story.

Why Bed Tax Is the Lead Revenue Metric

Among all the indicators on this dashboard, bed tax is the single most credible signal of tourism revenue. It captures total spending (rooms × occupancy × rate × spend), is collected and audited monthly by the Lee County Clerk of Court, and is reported on the same basis across municipalities — enabling apples-to-apples comparison between FMB, Sanibel, and the rest of Lee County. Inventory counts (hotel rooms, VR licenses) measure capacity; bed tax measures actual revenue.

Notes on the Numbers

Hotel room baseline

The Chamber's pre-Ian baseline of 1,724 rooms includes all properties that were operating immediately pre-Ian, plus the room counts at the 17 properties that were destroyed. The 1,306 current rooms includes the new 254-room Margaritaville Beach Resort, which opened post-Ian. At properties that existed pre-Ian, ~97% of room capacity is back online; the remaining gap is the 590 rooms at destroyed properties.

Hotel destruction

17 hotel properties were permanently destroyed by Hurricane Ian, totaling 590 rooms. Notable losses include Outrigger Beach Resort (144 rooms, sold to London Bay), Wyndham Garden (103), Sandpiper Gulf Resort (63), and Sandcastle Beach (53).

VR license interpretation

The 1,982 active vacation rental licenses (current) likely exceed the pre-Ian count — FMB's vacation rental market accelerated post-Ian as destroyed hotel rooms were replaced by short-term rentals. The 160 delinquent licenses are the more meaningful signal — properties that hold a license but cannot currently operate, typically because of ongoing damage or rebuild status.

Population

The 2024 ACS figure of 5,513 is close to the 2020 Census of 5,582, but the ACS also reports a 21.8% decline 2019–2024. This volatility reflects displacement and partial return, not stability. Population is shown as context, not as a recovery metric in the headline.

# Fort Myers Beach Recovery: Q1 2026 Baseline

### How We Track Hurricane Ian Recovery on Fort Myers Beach

---

## The Bottom Line

Two strong indicators, telling different parts of the same story:

- **Lodging capacity: 66% recovered** (4,159 of 6,263 pre-Ian rooms — FMB Chamber, March 2026)
- **Bed tax revenue: 48% recovered** (FY25 YTD through August vs FY22 YTD — Lee County Clerk)

The 18-point gap means capacity has rebuilt faster than per-room revenue — each rebuilt unit is generating less revenue than its pre-Ian counterpart.

| Sector | Lead Metric | Recovery |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **★ Lodging Capacity (the comprehensive figure)** | 4,159 of 6,263 pre-Ian rooms | **66%** |
| **★ Bed Tax (the revenue indicator)** | $3.85M of $8.04M pre-Ian YTD | **48%** |
| Lodging — Hotel Rooms | 1,306 of 1,724 pre-Ian rooms | **76%** |
| Lodging — Hotel Properties | 20 of 40 pre-Ian properties | **50%** |
| Lodging — Vacation Rental Rooms | 2,853 of 4,539 pre-Ian rooms | **63%** |
| Tourism — Attractions | 32 of 33 operational | **97%** |
| Economic — Restaurants | 47 of ~70 pre-Ian (estimate) | **~67%** |
| **Watchlist — Rooms permanently lost** | 816 of 6,263 pre-Ian | **13% gone forever** |
| Watchlist — Delinquent VR licenses | 160 of 2,142 total | 7.5% offline |
| Watchlist — Café concentration | 3 of 7 closed | 43% closure rate |

**Three-year bed-tax trajectory (YTD through August):** 15% (FY23) → 37% (FY24) → 48% (FY25). Rising steadily.

**Pipeline:** 1,303 lodging rooms in development across 14 named projects (942 hotel + 361 VR). Recovery is still in progress.

**FMB's distinctive recovery pattern:** vacation rentals (2,853 rooms) make up the majority of rebuilt capacity. The post-Ian lodging mix is more VR-tilted than the pre-Ian mix — a structural shift, not a temporary lag. This is why FMB is outpacing Sanibel on bed-tax recovery despite Sanibel having a stronger overall economic-recovery profile.

## Capacity vs Revenue — the 18-point gap

Lodging capacity 66% recovered, bed-tax revenue 48% recovered. The gap matters.

**What it means:** Each rebuilt room is generating less revenue than its pre-Ian counterpart. Possible drivers:

- **Lower occupancy** — visitor counts haven't fully returned; some rebuilt rooms sit empty more often
- **Lower nightly rates** — properties may be discounting to rebuild bookings, or replacing premium beachfront with mid-tier rebuilds
- **Mix shift** — vacation rentals carry lower per-room revenue than full-service resort hotels; FMB's recovery has been disproportionately VR-led

**What to watch:** if the gap closes over the next 4–8 quarters, the story is "recovery isn't done." If it persists, the story becomes "FMB has fewer high-revenue rooms than it used to" — a structural change, not a temporary one.

## Sister-City Context (FY25 YTD bed tax recovery)

Bed-tax recovery by municipality, all on the same Oct–Aug YTD frame:

| Municipality | Pre-Ian YTD | FY25 YTD | Recovery |
|--------------|------------:|---------:|---------:|
| **Fort Myers Beach** | $8,044,612 | $3,846,639 | **48%** |
| Sanibel | $7,177,273 | $2,252,421 | **31%** |
| Fort Myers (mainland, inland) | $4,317,947 | $6,181,640 | **143%** |
| Estero (inland) | $1,257,386 | $1,281,511 | **102%** |

The pattern is clear: barrier-island communities are still rebuilding; inland Lee County has not just recovered but exceeded pre-Ian revenue. FMB at 48% leads Sanibel at 31% — a function of FMB's faster vacation-rental rebuild relative to Sanibel's slower hotel reconstruction.

## Our Methodology

Adapted from the [Sanibel Solutions recovery framework](https://sanibel-solutions.com). The rules are deliberately strict to keep the dashboard credible.

**Five rules:**

1. **Compare each metric to its pre-Ian baseline** where the baseline has a verifiable source.
2. **Cap recovery at 100%** — a metric that exceeds its baseline is counted as fully recovered, not "120% recovered."
3. **Mark "baseline pending" when data is unverified.** Don't fabricate a number to fill a card.
4. **Exclude inverse indicators** — high real estate listing volume signals distress, not recovery.
5. **Surface leading indicators as Watchlist, not headline** — the 5 currently-closed businesses are a small denominator; the 160 delinquent VR licenses are the bigger story.

## What's Excluded (and Why)

| Excluded | Reason |
|----------|--------|
| Real estate sales / listings | Active market volume can indicate distress as easily as health (Sanibel's exclusion). |
| Building permits (issued / closed) | Measures reconstruction activity, not return to normalcy. |
| Population (as a recovery metric) | Flat year-over-year ACS numbers can hide significant displacement and re-arrival; shown as context only. |
| Open permits / backlog | Inverse indicator — high backlog is bad, not good. |

## Notes on the Numbers

**Hotel room baseline.** Multiple sources cite slightly different pre-Ian inventories — ~1,699 (Beach Talk Radio, Apr 2026, citing the Chamber) and ~2,000 (Beach Talk Radio, Mar 2025, including the not-yet-open Margaritaville). The dashboard uses 1,699 as the apples-to-apples baseline of rooms operating immediately pre-Ian. Margaritaville's 254 rooms have since opened and are included in the current 754 count.

**Hotel destruction.** Hurricane Ian destroyed 19 hotels on Fort Myers Beach outright (Apr 2026 reporting); earlier accounts cited 21 properties with 623 combined rooms. The lower count likely reflects converted properties (e.g., The Beacon, now a paid parking lot) being moved out of the "destroyed" category as their post-Ian status clarified.

**VR license interpretation.** The 1,982 active vacation rental licenses likely exceed the pre-Ian count. FMB's vacation rental market was already growing pre-Ian and accelerated post-Ian as destroyed hotel rooms were replaced by short-term rentals in rebuilt structures. The 160 delinquent licenses are the more meaningful signal — properties that hold a license but cannot currently operate, typically because of ongoing damage or rebuild status.

**Population.** The 2024 ACS figure of 5,513 is close to the 2020 Census of 5,582, but the ACS also reports a 21.8% decline 2019–2024. This volatility reflects displacement and partial return, not stability. Population is shown as context, not as a recovery metric in the headline.
